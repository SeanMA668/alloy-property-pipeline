# Composition sensitivity

How much one extra at.% of each element moves the solidus and the temperature at which the alloy splits into two BCC phases. Generated by `pipeline/sensitivity.py` from the Gaussian-process surrogates fitted on all 50 Thermo-Calc (TCHEA6 v6.1) results.

**Perturbation.** +1 at.% of one element, removed from the other four in proportion to their amounts. Intervals are 90 % ranges of the average change over 400 joint posterior draws of the GP. The last column repeats the calculation with a plain linear fit to the same Thermo-Calc values, as a check that the GP is not inventing the trend.

## Solidus (28 alloys that stay single-phase to 500 °C)

| +1 at.% of | Change (°C), GP | 90 % interval | Range across alloys | Linear fit on raw data |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| W | +8 | +7 to +9 | +5 to +12 | +8 |
| Ta | +5 | +4 to +5 | +2 to +10 | +5 |
| Mo | 0 | -1 to 0 | -3 to +4 | 0 |
| Nb | -4 | -5 to -3 | -5 to -2 | -4 |
| V | -18 | -19 to -17 | -20 to -11 | -18 |

**Reading:** W (+8 °C) and Ta (+5 °C) raise the solidus, V lowers it most (-18 °C), and Mo (0 °C) and Nb (-4 °C) matter little. The order follows the pure-element melting points (W 3422, Ta 3017, Mo 2623, Nb 2477, V 1910 °C): swapping in an element that melts above the alloy's own solidus raises it.

## Split temperature (22 alloys that split above 500 °C, 545–925 °C)

Only these alloys are used. The other 28 have a calculated split of "500 °C or lower" because the sweeps start at 500 °C, so the surrogate is flat there and its slope would average toward zero. The 22 split alloys lie on the two-phase side of the boundary, which is where a composition change decides whether an alloy passes.

| +1 at.% of | Change (°C), GP | 90 % interval | Range across alloys | Linear fit on raw data |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| V | +9 | +6 to +12 | 0 to +18 | +10 |
| Mo | +8 | +5 to +11 | -3 to +19 | +9 |
| Nb | -4 | -6 to -1 | -9 to +5 | -5 |
| W | -7 | -10 to -5 | -24 to 0 | -9 |
| Ta | -8 | -10 to -5 | -14 to +2 | -9 |

Positive means the alloy splits at a *higher* temperature, i.e. moves toward failing the single-phase goal.

**Reading:** adding V (+9 °C) and Mo (+8 °C) raises the split temperature; adding Ta (-8 °C), W (-7 °C) and Nb (-4 °C) lowers it.

## What this means for the design

- **V hurts both goals:** it lowers the solidus (-18 °C) and raises the split (+9 °C). This is the slope behind the pattern in the README (single-phase alloys average 9.6 at.% V, split alloys 23.1 %). The finalists keep V at 5.7–12.1 at.%.
- **Mo** barely moves the solidus (0 °C) but raises the split (+8 °C), so it is the second element to limit near the boundary.
- **W and Ta help both goals** (solidus +8 / +5 °C, split -7 / -8 °C) but are the densest elements (19.3 and 16.7 g/cm³), so they trade directly against weight; see `study_results.md`.
- **Nb** lowers the solidus a little (-4 °C) and the split a little (-4 °C) and is light (8.6 g/cm³), consistent with the lightest finalist, R5-4, being the Nb-rich one.
- These are local slopes, averaged over the alloys listed. They are not additive rules: the range column shows the same +1 at.% can do noticeably different things in different parts of composition space.

## Limits

- Surrogate slopes of a 50-point model, not Thermo-Calc calculations at perturbed compositions. A direct check would be Thermo-Calc runs at ±2 at.% around one finalist (planned as set C in the project status).
- The split temperature is censored at 500 °C, so nothing here says how far below 500 °C the single-phase alloys stay single-phase.
- Equilibrium only; the split is unlikely to occur at low temperature in practice because diffusion is slow (see `finalists.md`).
