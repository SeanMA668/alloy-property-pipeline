## How far to trust it

- **Yield strength:** 337 compression tests on single-phase BCC alloys from 53 chemical systems. Cross-validation grouped by system: R² 0.71, mean absolute error **202 MPa** (predicting the average every time: 414 MPa). Differences between candidates smaller than about 202 MPa are not meaningful.

  Measured, WMoTaNbV is stronger than WMoTaNb at every temperature. The model ranks them correctly at 23, 25, 600, 800, 1000 °C and in the wrong order at 1200, 1400, 1600 °C, and it narrows the measured gap. The same WMoTaNbV alloy is reported at 1246 and 2612 MPa at room temperature by different studies: measured strength depends strongly on processing and testing.
- **Reading the candidate table:** #1, #2, #3 and the control are predicted within a few tens of MPa of each other above 600 °C, well inside the model error, so the model cannot separate them on strength. #4 is predicted about 240 MPa weaker at 1000 °C, which is just above the error. Small non-monotonic steps with temperature (e.g. 600 vs 1000 °C) are noise from the forest, not physics.
- **Density:** rule of mixtures vs 31 measured densities: median error 0.3 %; for this system Mo1Nb1Ta1V1W1 12.36 vs 12.29 g/cm³, Mo1Nb1Ta1W1 13.64 vs 13.60 g/cm³. The largest errors (over 40 %) are Zn- and Ag-containing alloys, not relevant here.
- **Hardness was not usable:** 72 room-temperature BCC alloys, grouped cross-validation R² 0.30, error 76 HV vs 86 HV for the average. It is not reported for the candidates.
- **Not modelled:** ductility, oxidation resistance, creep. Refractory alloys of this family are known to be brittle at room temperature; strength alone does not make a usable alloy.
