# Testing Vegard's law with a machine-learned interatomic potential

Generated by `pipeline/mlip_strength.py` from `data/mlip/alloy_volumes.csv` (`pipeline/mlip_volumes.py`). Potential: MACE-MPA-0, medium (a universal machine-learned interatomic potential trained on DFT data). Each alloy is one random 128-atom BCC supercell with volume and atom positions relaxed at 0 K. 35 alloys and the 5 pure elements.

## 1. Pure elements: what the potential gets right and wrong

|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| W | 3.175 | 3.1652 | 589 / 256 / 142 | 523 / 204 / 161 |
| Mo | 3.151 | 3.1470 | 518 / 201 / 85 | 465 / 163 / 109 |
| Ta | 3.318 | 3.3058 | 244 / 183 / 38 | 261 / 157 / 82 |
| Nb | 3.317 | 3.3004 | 236 / 120 / -18 | 246 / 134 / 29 |
| V | 2.979 | 3.0240 | 271 / 142 / 6 | 231 / 120 / 43 |

Lattice parameters are within about 1 % (V is 1.5 % too small). Elastic constants are close for W and Mo but far off for the shear constant C44 of Ta, Nb and V (Nb's is negative, i.e. mechanically unstable). Plain DFT is known to have trouble with C44 of Nb and V, and this potential is trained on DFT data. So the strength model keeps experimental elastic constants, and the potential is used only for volumes, where it is reliable and where the model assumes Vegard's law.

## Reading

The potential finds that adding V expands these alloys more than Vegard's law predicts (every pair term with V is positive), so V behaves as a larger atom in the alloy and its misfit volume shrinks by about a fifth. Because the model's strength scales with misfit volume to the 4/3 power, the corrected strengths fall and the validation error grows from 154 to 303 MPa. The measured lattice parameters do not settle which is right: Vegard's law fits them as well as or better than the corrected values, and the scatter between studies is as large as the difference. The project keeps Vegard misfit volumes, as the published model does. Two findings stand: the strength predictions are sensitive to V's effective size in the alloy (in the three V-containing validation alloys the corrected strength is 20–23 % lower), and the finalist ranking does not depend on that choice.

## Caveats

- One random configuration per alloy (no special quasirandom structures, no averaging over configurations); 0 K volumes, no thermal expansion.
- The potential is trained on DFT data and inherits its errors; using only its deviation from Vegard's law reduces, but does not remove, that dependence.
- The quadratic fit is valid inside the sampled composition range. The potential's pure V is 1.5 % too small, so its description of V, the element that matters most here, is the least certain.
- Measured lattice parameters exist for only four alloys, and studies of the same alloy differ by up to 0.007 Å.
